--- layout: layout.html title: Team Card description: "The night's card: lineups, every frame, and the two signatures." extraStyles: - /stylesheets/league.css extraScripts: - /scripts/match-settings.js - /scripts/league-nav.js - /scripts/league-card.js --- {% comment %} The captain's scorecard. A full recording surface in its own right, not management around the match page: a captain can run the whole night from this grid without /match/ existing at all. What keeps the one-page rule honest is that there is one frame set, not one screen — this page and the match page read and write the same frames. In the order the night happens (UV's layout, 24 Oct): 1. A banner: the two teams with their shields, the card's state as a badge, and the fixture's details (league, date, time, tables, team size, reserves, sessions, frames). 2. The squad and the order, until the side locks. The order is one player per position (five in a five-man league); the night is built from the two orders and the rotation table at the second lock. It is a blind exchange — the other side's names are withheld by tdc_match_lineup until both have locked, so nothing here can leak them. 3. The sessions, once the frames exist. Each frame a row: the home player, an L (who won the lag) and a W (who won the frame) three-way switch, home · nobody · away, the away player; the session's totals under it. Tapping a player's name on an unplayed frame opens the reserve picker, and a reserve wears a badge. A seat left empty waits for a reserve or a latecomer until a captain signs, then is a walkover. 4. Every player's frames: head to head (a table per team), or session by session. 5. The result: the two teams and their scores, in a banner again. 6. The signatures, laid out as a form waiting to be signed. Reached as /leagues/card/?m=. {% endcomment %}
