## Data architecture
- One Supabase project for everything, never one project per venue.
- **`tbl_players` is the hub.** One row per person, with or without a login:
  - `user_id` links to `auth.users`. It can be empty and is unique. It's empty for guests and for walk-ins who haven't signed up.
  - `player_type` is `member`, `walk_in` or `guest`. `guest_of_player_id` records whose guest someone is.
  - Signups create a `member` row automatically (trigger). A walk-in claims their existing row later, through the identifiers step.
  - Only non-private info goes here (display name, first and last name), and all signed-in users can read it.
- **Private identifiers** (SA ID, passport, phone, email) go in a separate protected table, never in `tbl_players`.
  - Verification belongs to the ID, and is done in store by staff (verified_by, verified_at).
  - Blocking rules per business track are enforced in the database, not only in the UI. Cannabis: everything is blocked until verified. Pool: league entry requires verification.
- Domains:
  - **Global:** Identity, Ranking & Competition (WST-style points), Gamification (XP, raffles, fantasy league, hall of fame, top trumps).
  - **Venue-scoped via `venue_id`:** Commerce (closed-loop credits, working name "Cuedits"), Venue Ops (bookings, stock, table scheduling).
  - **Cross-cutting:** Platform (audit log, roles).
- Commerce and Ranking never share tables. `player_id` on a transaction is nullable (cash sales with no linked member are valid).

## Venues (migration `20261105200430`, 5 Nov; step 1 of 3: the database. Step 2, the picker, below. Step 3 is to come)

**A competition has two kinds of venue, kept apart on purpose.**

| | Belongs to | Played at |
|---|---|---|
| Held in | `venue_id` on `tbl_leagues`, `tbl_tournaments` | `tbl_league_season_venues`, `tbl_tournament_venues`, `tbl_league_fixtures.venue_id` |
| Gives | Its staff run it; its credits settle there | Nothing: words on a page |
| Who chooses | League: the venue's owner or admin. Tournament: owner, admin or staff | Whoever runs the competition, any active venue, no approval |
| Changes | Fixed at creation | Any time |

- **`tbl_matches.venue_id` is a rights column.** A match copies what its competition belongs to, and `private.tdc_can_edit_match` lets that venue's staff edit the card. A played-at venue must never be written there, or a barman at a club hosting one night could change a league's scores.
- **The picker (`scripts/venue-picker.js`, `TDCVenuePicker`, step 2):** a search box that adds each venue as a pill, for the played-at list only, on `/tournaments/new/`, `/leagues/new/` and both Settings tabs. It filters `tdc_venues()` in the browser with no call, by name, area and city. The order is the order added. The list is saved by its own call after the rest, and only when it changed, so an old competition is never rewritten by an unrelated save. A closed venue is not in `tdc_venues()` and cannot be named by someone who is not its staff, so it keeps a pill reading "A closed venue": a save that left it out would take it off without anyone choosing that. A new season starts from the last season's list, closed venues left out, which is what `tdc_create_league_season` copies; the list is sent only if the person changed it. On a create page the competition exists by the time the venues are saved, so a refusal leaves the button off and links to Settings rather than letting a second press make another. **The venue a competition belongs to has no picker yet: `p_venue_id` is still always null.** Nothing shows the played-at list yet; the season banner and the poster still read the belongs-to venue.
- **Played-at is per season**, so a league moving club does not rewrite its old seasons. `tdc_create_league_season` copies the last season's list, less any venue since closed. `tdc_set_league_season_venues` and `tdc_set_tournament_venues` take the whole list in order; empty clears it. A venue that closed while on a list may stay on it.
- **A fixture** may name its own venue (`p_venue_id`, `p_clear_venue` on `tdc_set_league_fixture`; `p_venue_id` on `tdc_add_league_fixture`). `tdc_league_fixtures` returns `venue_id`, `venue_name` and `venue_inherited`: its own, else the season's when the season has exactly one. A blank fixture in a season at several returns none, so the page lists the season's rather than sending a player to the wrong club.
- **Details on `tbl_venues`:** description, address, area, city, maps_url, picture_path, table_count, table_note, phone, whatsapp, email, website_url, instagram_url, facebook_url, hours_note. Links must be https, which keeps a `javascript:` address off public pages.
- **Hours are `tbl_venue_hours`:** one row each time the venue opens (`day_of_week` 1 is Monday, 7 Sunday; `opens_at`, `closes_at`). No row is closed. A closing time earlier than the opening time is the next morning. **No function writes these**: UV types them in the dashboard's Table Editor.
- **Reading:** `tdc_venues()` (the active list) and `tdc_venue(id)` (one venue with its hours) are the only way in for someone signed out; `tbl_venues` is not granted to `anon`. Display details only.
- **Editing:** `tdc_update_venue` (the venue's owner or admin; the whole form at once, so a blank clears) and `tdc_set_venue_picture`. Name, slug, timezone, currency and active are not editable there. **New venues come through a migration.** Nobody can edit a venue with no owner or tie a competition to it, but anyone may list it as played at.
- **Picture:** the public `venue-pictures` bucket, `<venue id>/<random>.webp`, built as `team-pictures` is.
- **Where a venue is shown (migration `20261105203829`, 5 Nov; step 3).** Everything shows where a competition is **played**, as `[{ id, name }]` in the director's order: a `venues` column on `tdc_my_leagues`, `tdc_search_leagues`, `tdc_league_season_info`, `tdc_my_tournaments` and `tdc_open_tournaments` (their old `venue_id` and `venue_name` still mean what it belongs to, and no page reads them now); `venues` in the poster's snapshot and on a profile's teams and matches; `tdc_tournament_venue_list(id)` for pages reading the tournament's own row; `tdc_match_venue_list(id)` for one match: a fixture's own venue, else the season's list, a tournament's list, or a social match's venue. Changing a tournament's list rebuilds its poster, if it has one, with the choices it already has, since Settings saves the list after everything else.
- **Drawn by four helpers in `tdc.js`, so every page reads the same:** `venueNames` (words, on a card that is itself a link), `venueLink`, `venueHeading` (a heading's line) and `venueFact` (a banner's row, a line each, null with none). With no venue the row is left out everywhere. The banner on a match counts several ("3 venues").
- **A venue's page** (`/venues/?v=<id>`, `scripts/venue.js`): renders signed out, because the poster links to it. Reads `tdc_venue` and `tdc_venue_competitions`, the second listing what belongs to the venue, is played at it, or (leagues) has a fixture there, and only what is listed. A league opens its join page; a tournament its poster until it starts, then its draw. Those but the poster ask a stranger to sign in.
- **Not settled by this:** travelling-league fees (`STATUS.md` §5). A league with no owning venue still has nowhere for fees to go.
- **Seeded:** The Dive Club (address from the footer, the rest to come), Billiard Café (Rivonia) and Billiard Café (Randpark Ridge) from the Google Maps listings UV sent. Postcodes are not held.

## The install tutorial (`/install/`, 3 Nov)

How to put the app on a home screen, shown rather than told. UV's decisions are in `docs/DECISIONS.md` (3 Nov); the draft UV reviewed is the claude.ai artifact "Install on iPhone".

- **One section per kind of device (13 Nov).** `install.js` shows the one `TDCDevice.kind` names (below), or `?for=<kind>`; "Not your phone?" lists the others. Safari on an iPhone is the drawn one; iPhone Chrome, Edge and Firefox, Android (with the one-tap **Install the app** button while the browser offers it, and every browser's menu steps), the "open this in your browser" page for links opened inside Instagram or Facebook (Open in Chrome on Android, the app's menu steps and Copy the link), the installed app and a computer are written steps until UV's screenshots are drawn. Both iPhone sections end with "sign in once more". The Safari animation runs only while its section shows, and Compact's folded heading applies only there (the drawing sits one level deeper now, so `applyMode` finds the page with `closest('.install')`).
- **The drawing** (`_includes/install/safari.svg`, included into the page): every screen redrawn in SVG from UV's iOS 26 screenshots, in the iPhone's own 390 × 844 points. Contacts, apps, the widget and the wallpaper are plain shapes; the clock always reads 16:20 and the battery 80. It points at the site's own `apple-touch-icon.png` (the icon iOS really uses: see-through, so the share sheet shows it on white and the home screen on black, as on a real phone), `icon-primary.svg` and `logo-primary-dark.svg`, so **a new icon or logo arrives by itself**. The site's home page is one block, `#ins-tdc-home`: **when the home page changes, redraw that block**. Every id starts `ins-` and every class `i-`, so the site's styles never reach it.
- **The drawing follows the theme** (UV, 3 Nov): iOS in dark mode in the dark theme, in light mode in the light theme. Every colour in it is a token in `install.css` (`--ios-*` for the iPhone, `--ins-*` for the site's screen, whose band, rings, words and button use the site's own `--bg-base`, `--color-primary`, `--text-primary`), written into the SVG as inline styles, never as `fill="var(…)"` attributes (a presentation attribute is not sure to read a variable in every browser). Seven colours are fixed on purpose, the same in both: the white tiles behind app icons, the toggle's knob, the white words on the blue Add and the site's button (as the real page has), the black dim and the black under the home-screen icon. The site's screen is a `<use>` copy, which the site's `.for-dark` / `.for-light` rule cannot reach, so its two logo pairs show by `display: var(--ins-show-dark / -light)`. The app icon stays the dark one in both: a phone's home-screen icon does not follow a site's theme. Checked 3 Nov: the dark theme's phone pixel-identical to before at 24 moments of the loop; the light theme looked at through every step.
- **The animation** (`scripts/install.js`): the timeline is a set of numbers keyed in time (the finger's position and press, how far the keyboard, menu, sheet and dialog are in, the toggle, the icon's pop), eased between keys; `render(t)` draws any moment from them. That is what lets a tap on a step jump straight to it and the loop restart cleanly. A flat dot in the site's green is the finger: it moves, taps (a ripple), drags the sheet up by its header (never on a button) and types the address one key at a time. **The finger's points are coordinates in the drawing**: move a button in the SVG and move its point in the timeline with it.
- **The steps**: the typing is an unnumbered lead-in ("Already here in Safari? Start at 1"), because anyone reading the page is already on the site; numbered steps 1–5 start at ⋯. The list is built from the same array the timeline uses.
- **Full and Compact**, switched by the pill beside Play and animated between (the View Transitions API; instant where the browser has none or with less motion). **Compact** is for following along on the phone itself: an 84px-wide screen and the live step beside it, held under the nav (`position: sticky`), the heading folded away so the block sits right under the nav (the page is too short to scroll it there). About 198px tall, because Safari's half-open Share sheet leaves about 211px of page under the nav, measured from UV's screenshots (the sheet's top at 329 of 844 points). The list scrolls so the live step sits at the top of its window. The page opens in Full until switched (UV); the choice is kept on the device (`tdc.install-mode`).
- **Less motion** (the profile menu's toggle or the phone's setting, `TDCPrefs.lessMotion`): the animation waits for Play.
- `?t=<seconds>` and `?mode=compact` hold a moment, for headless layout checks.

## Getting the app (13 Nov)

UV asked for prompts that guide new players to install the app. **UV's rules:** phones only; never in the installed app; the strip shows on every page from a visit to the home page until ✕, then stays away until the next visit to the home page (a first visit anywhere shows it too); a "Get the app" row always in the menu; no pop-up. The look and the words are Claude's, for UV to refine.

**What can be automated, and what cannot:**

| Where | What the site does | Left to the person |
|---|---|---|
| Android in Chrome, Samsung Internet or Edge | The browser's install offer (`beforeinstallprompt`) is held, so the browser's own banner never shows, and the strip's **Install** opens the phone's dialog | One tap: Install |
| iPhone, any browser | No way exists for a site to install itself; `/install/` shows the steps | Share → Add to Home Screen → Add |
| Inside Instagram, Facebook, TikTok… | Nothing can be installed there. On Android, Open in Chrome (an `intent://` address); on an iPhone, the app's menu steps and Copy the link | Opening the browser, then the steps |
| The installed app | Never prompted. A one-off welcome instead | Signing in again (iPhone), turning notifications on |

**`scripts/device.js` (`TDCDevice`)**, in the `<head>` with no dependencies, so it hears Android's offer however early it comes. Reads the user agent: `os`, `browser`, `inApp`, `mobile`, `installed` (`display-mode: standalone`, or Safari's `navigator.standalone`), `kind` (`installed`, `in-app`, `ios-safari`, `ios-other`, `android`, `desktop`), `canInstall`, `install()`, `openInBrowserUrl()`; events `tdc:installable` and `tdc:installed`. An iPad on iPadOS says it is a Mac, so a Mac with a touch screen counts as `ios`. An iPhone app's web view does not say `Safari/`, which every real iPhone browser does; the installed app does not either, hence the installed check first. The offer is ignored on an iPhone or in an app's browser (headless desktop Chrome posing as an iPhone received one). Only for choosing steps: never to refuse anybody anything. Nav's older `runningInstalled()` (the Reload button) was left as it was.

**The strip** (`#nav-get-app` in `_includes/nav.html`, `nav.css`, the "Getting the app" part of `nav.js`): in the page's flow under the bar, so it pushes the page down and scrolls away with it. `tdc.get-app-off` in `localStorage` is ✕; the home page clears it. Words by case: Android with the offer, "one tap" and **Install** (switching from How when the offer arrives late); in an app's browser, "Open this in your browser"; otherwise "Get the app on your home screen" and **How** to `/install/`. Not on `/install/` itself. **The menu row** (`#nav-get-app-row`, the new `install` ring icon in `ring-status.js`) shows on a phone in a browser.

**The installed app's welcome**, the same strip: signed out, "sign in once more" (an iPhone's installed app keeps its own sign-in, apart from Safari's) and Sign in; signed in, "turn on notifications" to the profile's `#notifications` while the phone has not been asked (`Notification.permission` is `default`). `tdc.app-welcomed` ends it: ✕, or the phone having been asked.

**The profile's notifications on an iPhone in a browser** (`push.js`): an iPhone sends a website's notifications only to the installed app, so the button reads "Get the app first" and opens `/install/`, the hint says why, and the test button is hidden.

**The record** (migration `20261113164109_player_devices`, test `075_player_devices`): signed in, the nav calls `tdc_record_device(os, browser, installed)` once a day per person, device, browser and app-or-not (`tdc.device-seen` holds the last). `tbl_player_devices` has one row per player and `(os, browser, installed)`: `first_seen`, `last_seen`, `days_seen` (Johannesburg days). An unknown value is refused, so a new browser name in `device.js` must be added to the function and the table's check too; a failure is a console error only. No grants and no policies: read in the SQL editor until the points read it (`docs/systems/points.md`). `private.tdc_merge_players` moves the account's rows onto the record that stays. Nothing is recorded for a visitor who is not signed in.

## Short links (9 Nov, being built in steps)

The design is `docs/design/short-links.md`. Step 1, the database, is migration `20261109114638`; no page uses it yet.

**Players (10 Nov; migration `20261110084238`, test `067_player_short_links`):** `tbl_players.short_code` holds the whole link as shown (`Yuvan-Naidoo-2`), unlike a competition's code, because a player's letters and number are not added at display. The browser has no grant on the column, so it cannot be written from a page. A `before insert or update of first_name, last_name, user_id` trigger (`private.tdc_player_short_code_on_save`) builds it, so the profile page, the sign-up and the walk-in merge needed no change. The same name saved again keeps its number; a new name takes the lowest free one, under an advisory lock on the name, because a clash on the unique index would fail somebody's sign-up. The index is on `upper(short_code)`, as the lookup holds a code in capitals. `tdc_resolve_short_code` tries digits, then `T-` and `L-`, and only then a name; signed out it answers `{reason: 'sign_in'}` for any name-shaped address before looking, and `short-link.js` sends them through `TDC.requireSession`, which brings them back to the link. `tdc_short_link('player', id)` refuses a signed-out caller, so an id cannot be turned into a name; its `code` is null when there is no link, which is why `TDC.shareButton` takes `longUrl`.

**Names at sign-up (10 Nov; migration `20261110093122`, test `068_signup_names`):** the email form sends `first_name` and `last_name` in the sign-up's metadata, optional, and `tdc_create_player_for_new_user` saves them, so the link exists from the first moment. Google sends only `full_name`, in one string, which cannot be split safely, so the trigger leaves it alone. `auth.js` `goOn` sends anybody signed in with a name missing to `/accounts/names/` (`account-names.js`), keeping `next`; Skip goes on and saves nothing, and with no record of the skip it asks again at the next sign-in. Its check runs after `onAuthStateChange`'s callback (a `setTimeout`), because a database call from inside that callback never comes back.

- **Stored:** `short_code` and `short_code_by` on `tbl_tournaments` and `tbl_leagues` (only the chosen part: the `T-`, `L-` and dashes are added when shown, so a code cannot clash with an address); `match_number` on both fixture tables; `short_number` on `tbl_matches`, shown only for a social match.
- **Why the number is on the fixture:** a league or tournament match is only tied to its competition when the fixture's `match_id` is written, just after the match is made. A trigger on that write takes the next number from the competition's `next_match_number`, so no function that creates a match had to change, and locking the competition's row stops two matches getting one number.
- **Functions:** `tdc_resolve_short_code` (anon too: gives back `{path}` or `{reason}`), `tdc_short_link` (anon too: the code to show), `tdc_short_code_status` (the field's live feedback), `tdc_set_short_code`.
- **Security:** a path is only ever built in the database from an id, so a code cannot lead off the site. Reasons never name the competition. `short_code_by` references `auth.users`, not `tbl_players`, so the walk-in merge is unaffected.

**Step 2, following a link (written 9 Nov):** `404.html` loads `scripts/short-link.js`, which calls `tdc_resolve_short_code` and does `location.replace` to the answer, or shows the reason. It works because GitHub Pages, and the local dev server, send `404.html` for any address that does not exist. Every address on that page must start at the root, since it is served at any depth. `layout.html` takes an optional `ogUrl`, so this page's preview names the site and not `/404.html`. `sw.js` no longer caches a navigation that was not a success, and answers a code-shaped address opened offline with a small inline page, because `404.html`'s scripts and styles are not cached.

**Step 3, setting the code (written 9 Nov):** `scripts/short-code-field.js` (`TDCShortCode.create(box, area, kind, id)`, then `load()`) draws the section on both Settings tabs, so it behaves the same on each. It has its own Save, because a change is live at once, unlike the tab's Save. A league's tab passes kind `season`, as that is the id the page holds. Styles are `.short-code-*` in `forms.css`.

**Step 4, sharing (written 9 Nov):** `TDC.shareButton(button, kind, id, { area, status, title })` in `tdc.js`. The link is read when the button is made and the button waits for it, because a phone only opens its share sheet from inside the tap itself: reading the code after the tap would be refused. A page's button is made once; the match page's are made afresh each time its ⋯ menu or result is drawn, so the code is kept for the page's life. Kinds: `tournament`, `season`, `match`, `player`.

**Step 5, the links shown (written 9 Nov):** the league's and tournament's "The link to share" boxes read `TDC.shortUrl`, each time the tab loads, as a code changed on Settings must show at once. The Poster button's own `href` stays long: nobody sees it. A player's profile keeps its long link: only leagues, tournaments and matches have codes.

## Full names: the surname lighter (12 Nov)

**The rule (UV, 12 Nov):** wherever a player's first name and surname are shown, the surname is drawn at **400** and the first name keeps the weight of whatever holds it (600 in a Standings row, 700 on a won draw card). **Display names are never split**: a display name is the player's own choice and often a nickname. Where a full name already sits at 400 (a muted sub-line under a display name), nothing looks different; the markup is there so the rule still holds if that line's weight changes.

**How:** `TDC.fullName(full, surname)` in `tdc.js` returns a fragment, the first name as text and the surname in `<span class="tdc-surname">` (`global.css`). `TDC.fullNameOf(first, last)` does the same from a row's two names. Both go in with `append` / `replaceChildren`, never `textContent`. If the surname does not end the full name (both come from the same row, so it should never happen) the name is drawn whole and `[Names]` goes to the console.

**Why the surname comes separately:** the server joined first name and surname into one `full_name` string, and splitting it again at a space is wrong for a first name such as "Mary Anne". Migration `20261112152540_surnames_apart` adds `surname` beside `full_name` to every read that sends one: `tdc_search_opponents`, `tdc_search_entrants`, `tdc_search_league_players`, `tdc_search_referees`, `tdc_my_follows`, `tdc_league_standings` (each player), `tdc_player_profile` (the player), and `home_surname` / `away_surname` at the end of `tdc_tournament_draw`. The table-returning ones were dropped and created again (patched from a freshly reset database's text, their grants and comments restored); the two jsonb ones were replaced in place. It is called `surname`, not `last_name`, so an output column can never be confused with `tbl_players.last_name` inside the function. Reads that already sent `first_name` and `last_name` (the entrant lists, the nav's own player, `/match/`'s players, the staff search) use `fullNameOf`. Test: `074_surnames_apart`.

**Where it is drawn:** the Standings and draw Full names switch (and the draw's entrant pills), league and tournament management's player searches and the league's entrant lists, the opponent and referee pickers, `/match/`'s players, the player profile (header, About, Compare, Find, Following), the profile menu's banner, the staff pages' player line, and the walk-ins on a profile.

**A limit:** `TDC.liveFade` skips an element that holds other elements, so a full name in the draw does not fade when its slot changes; the display name still does.
