# Tournaments (migration `20261019140000`)

> Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 23 Oct 2026.

- **The governing requirement: a tournament director must be able to run the whole thing alone.** Players entering their own scores is a convenience, not the path. Plenty of entrants never open the app, and a TD at a club night works from a scoresheet on the bar. Everything in this area is shaped by that, and a design that assumes player participation is wrong.
- **An entrant is a player record, which may be a walk-in with no account.** Nobody has to sign up to be in a tournament. This is why entrants key on `player_id` and not `user_id`.
- `tbl_tournaments` — identity, `rules` (the default ruleset matches inherit, validated by the same `private.tdc_check_match_rules` the match page uses, so a tournament cannot hold a ruleset its own matches would refuse), `format` (stored, not yet acted on), status, fee, `max_entrants`. `venue_id` is **nullable on purpose**: anyone may run a tournament, and an approved club gets branding and venue features — the tier falls out of that column being empty or not. `sanctioned` is a column, never a ruleset key.
- `tbl_competition_roles` + `tbl_competition_role_types` — **a lookup table with a rank number, not an enum**, so "outranks" is a comparison rather than a chain of ifs. `tournament_id` is nullable because a league season gets its own column here later, which is also why it is not called `tournament_role`.
- `private.tdc_can_run_tournament` is **the single place deciding who runs a competition**, in the same spirit as `private.tdc_can_edit_match`. Director, or venue staff where the tournament belongs to a venue — because a club night's TD is often whoever is behind the bar, and a club must be able to rescue a tournament whose director went home.
- **Whoever creates a tournament is made its director in the same transaction**, or nobody can change it.
- `tdc_set_seeds` takes the **whole order in one call** and clears anyone not named. A re-seed moves everybody, and half a re-seed is a draw nobody can read.
- `tdc_tournament_entrants` returns names, status, paid and seed together — the TD's entrant list is the screen a tournament is actually run from, and a request per row on a page showing thirty is not a page.
- **Not here, deliberately:** rounds, brackets and progression. Setup captures identity, rules and entrants; none of those depend on the format, and generating the draw is its own step. That separation is what let the setup page exist before any bracket generator does.
- Still to come: the format's draw generator, match-level score entry (a TD-run match may have **no frames at all**, so an entered score is a normal recording path rather than an override), and coordinator approval — see `claude/approval-and-corrections.md`.

### The setup pages (migration `20261019150000`)
- Three pages, following the shape of `/matches/`: the list, the create page, and the entrant list at `/tournaments/manage/?t=<id>`.
- **`tdc_search_entrants` is a second search, not a change to `tdc_search_opponents`.** The opponent search returns players with a login only and leaves the caller out of their own results, which is right for "who am I playing" and wrong twice over for a tournament: entrants are routinely walk-ins, and a TD plays in their own club night. The two questions have different answers, so they are two functions. The new one is gated on `private.tdc_can_run_tournament`, because unlike the opponent search it returns walk-in records — people who never chose to be findable.
- **Seeding is always sent as the whole order.** The seed boxes are read, sorted (unseeded falling to the end in name order), and the result handed to `tdc_set_seeds` as seeds 1..n. Typing a duplicate therefore cannot produce one — the second entrant just sorts next to the first. Randomise is Fisher-Yates, not a `Math.random()` sort, which is not uniform.
- **Withdrawn entrants stay on the list and out of the draw.** They keep their row so the TD can see who pulled out, they are never seeded, and they do not count toward `max_entrants`. Removing is different and asks first: it takes the person out of the record entirely.
- After every write the list is read again rather than patched in the page, as everywhere else in this project — the server owns the ordering.
- Read access and write access differ here: anyone signed in may read an entrant list, only the people running it may change one. The page settles that **before the first render** (a `readOnly` flag every render reads) so the controls are never offered and then taken away. The database refuses the write either way; this is the courtesy, not the guard.
- **Known gaps in these pages:**
  - No venue picker, so `p_venue_id` is always null and every tournament made here is the open tier. A club tournament needs the picker and staff rights at that venue.
  - A walk-in cannot be created *from* the entrant page, but the flow exists and is correct: `tdc_create_walk_in` needs venue staff rights, while `tdc_add_entrant` needs only that you run the tournament and the player exists. So staff-anywhere creates the walk-in at their own venue and then adds them to any tournament they run, venue or not. The only case that cannot is a TD who is staff at no venue at all, and per the verification ladder a level-0 player record *should* only be created by somebody accountable. **Resolved 19 Oct, UV: not a gap.** What is left is walking — `/staff/walk-in/` should accept `?next=` (via the existing `TDC.safeNext`) so creating a walk-in returns to the entrant list.
  - Nothing edits a tournament after creation, though `tdc_update_tournament` exists for it.
  - Entrants are not notified that they have been entered.

### The draw (migration `20261019160000`)
- **Format decision, 19 Oct (UV):** single elimination first. Round robin is more useful for leagues and comes with them. Double elimination follows, and is mostly a second set of source rows rather than a second generator — `loser_of` already exists in the design, and a losers'-bracket match is just a match whose slots are `loser_of` two others. The drop pattern is the real work there, not the schema.
- **`tbl_tournament_fixtures` holds the shape of the draw, not the matches.** The progression design put `home_source_type` / `home_source_ref` on the match; they cannot go there, because `tbl_matches_home_side_is_one_thing` requires a `player` side to carry a `player_id` and a round-of-16 match between two unknown people therefore cannot exist. Relaxing that constraint would weaken a guard protecting every social and league match in order to serve tournaments only. **Decided 19 Oct (UV): a separate table.** A real `tbl_matches` row is created later, when both sides are known — so a bracket is readable and publishable with no matches in existence, which is what a draw sheet on the wall actually is.
- **No winner column, and the reason matters.** A played fixture carries `match_id` and its winner is counted from that match's frames like everybody else's. A bye, a walkover or a withdrawal is an advancement with *nothing to count* — no frames, no match — so those are recorded in `advanced_entrant_id` + `advance_reason`. A check constraint makes the two mutually exclusive, which is what keeps "frames are the only record" true in the presence of byes.
- `bracket` is `main` / `losers` / `third_place` from the start. The losers' bracket is the next format, and a fixture that could not say which bracket it belongs to would need a migration against every row to acquire one.
- **Rounds number up from 1**, so the final is the highest number. Numbering down from the final would renumber every fixture the moment the field changes size.
- `private.tdc_bracket_order` builds the standard draw sheet by reflection: `[1]`, then at each doubling each seed sits next to the one completing the new size. Size 8 comes out `[1,8,4,5,2,7,3,6]`. This is why seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final, and why byes land on the top seeds.
- **`tdc_generate_draw` settles the seeding rather than demanding it first.** Entrants are taken in seed order with anybody unseeded falling to the end by name, and those positions are written back as seeds 1..n — so the seeds always describe the draw actually generated, and a TD who never touched seeding still gets a bracket.
- `p_confirmed_only` decides the field: confirmed only, or everybody not withdrawn. **Decided 19 Oct (UV): the TD chooses at draw time**, because the honest answer depends on whether the people who said "interested" turned up.
- `third_place` is a column on `tbl_tournaments`, off by default — a property of the tournament, never a ruleset key. Generated as one fixture fed by `loser_of` both semi-finals, and only where there are two semi-finals to lose.
- **A started draw can still be redrawn or cleared — it just asks first.** *(Changed 19 Oct on UV's instruction: the TD has full control over their own tournament.)* With matches attached, `tdc_generate_draw` and `tdc_clear_draw` refuse once with a message naming how many, and go ahead when passed `p_detach_matches`. Nothing is destroyed in either case: the FK runs from the fixture to the match, so deleting fixtures leaves the played matches intact — they simply stop belonging to a bracket.
- **`progression` on `tbl_tournaments` is `automatic` or `manual`.** Automatic puts a finished match's winner in the slot above; manual moves nobody and the TD places each one. **It governs only whether the system moves people *for* the TD, never whether the TD may move them** — plenty of club tournaments are run loose, with progression adjusted by hand as the night goes, and a bracket that argued with the person running it would just be wrong.
- **`tdc_set_fixture` is that control.** It sets either slot, empties either slot, or awards the fixture to somebody without it being played (walkover, withdrawal, decision). It works on a started draw and in both progression modes. It refuses only what would make a bracket unreadable: an entrant from another tournament, somebody playing themselves, an award to a player who is not in the fixture, an award with no reason, and an award on a fixture that has a match — that one is counted from its frames.
  - **Filling a slot by hand clears its source.** A slot fed by `winner_of` that the TD has filled stops being fed, or the next automatic advance would quietly overwrite them. Emptying a slot leaves it with no source at all, which is the same shape a bye is written in.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres: the redraw confirmation and every `tdc_set_fixture` refusal, and across field sizes 2 to 33: fixture count is always size−1, byes always size−n and always on the top seeds, and with favourites advancing an 11-player draw still gives semi-finals of 1v4 and 2v3 and a final of 1v2.

### Progression (migration `20261019170000`)
- **`create_matches` on `tbl_tournaments` is `automatic` or `manual`** — the match behind a fixture is made the moment both sides are known, or when the TD says a table is free. *(19 Oct, UV: a feature, not a question.)* Same shape as `progression`: it governs what the system does on its own, never what the TD may do by hand.
- **`private.tdc_fixture_outcome` is the single answer to who won a fixture**, returning winner *and* loser as entrant ids. The loser is returned because `loser_of` feeds both the third-place playoff and the whole losers' bracket, which would otherwise each work it out again.
  - Awarded (bye, walkover, withdrawal, decision) → read off the fixture, since there are no frames to count.
  - Played → the score is counted from `tbl_frames` exactly as `tdc_my_matches` counts it. Never read from a column.
  - It advances nobody when the match is still in progress, or when the card is level: `draws_allowed` is off for knockout play and `tiebreak_method` settles it at the table, so a level finished card means the tiebreak has not been recorded, and the bracket should show that rather than guess.
- **`tdc_resolve_draw` is idempotent and called by the page**, on load and after every write, like every other read in this project. It is deliberately *not* a trigger: a resolver firing from a trigger advances somebody in the middle of a correction, which is the exact moment a TD is trying to stop it. One pass walks rounds in order, so a round-1 win can reach round 3 without three calls.
- **A slot the TD filled by hand is never overwritten.** `tdc_set_fixture` sets its source to `entrant`, and the resolver only fills slots still sourced `winner_of` / `loser_of`. Tested: a TD putting a beaten player into the final keeps them there while the other slot still fills automatically.
- `tdc_create_fixture_match` freezes the tournament's ruleset onto the match the same way `tdc_create_match` does, and is idempotent — called twice it returns the match it already made. The tournament's `sanctioned` flag propagates to its matches: an authorisation fact in both places, never decided again per match.
- `tdc_detach_fixture_match` takes a match off a fixture without deleting it. The match is a real record of frames really played; deleting one is `tdc_delete_match`'s job, asked for explicitly.
- `tdc_tournament_draw` was **dropped and recreated** rather than replaced — it gained columns, and PostgreSQL will not change a function's return type in place. It now also returns the frame score, the match status, the winner, and the card state, so a bracket can show that somebody advanced while their opponent is still disputing the card.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres by playing whole tournaments: an 8-player bracket round by round to a winner; an 11-player field where all 5 byes advance on the first resolve; manual progression moving nobody; automatic progression with manual creation filling slots but starting nothing; a level card and an in-progress card each advancing nobody; and third place correctly fed by the two semi-final losers.

### The bracket page (migration `20261019180000`)
- `tournaments/bracket/index.html` + `scripts/tournament-bracket.js` + `stylesheets/bracket.css`, at `?t=<tournament id>`. Readable by any signed-in player; the controls appear only for the people running it, settled before the first render.
- **`tdc_update_tournament` was dropped and recreated** to carry `third_place`, `progression` and `create_matches`. Without it the three columns added earlier today had no way to be set and every tournament was stuck on the defaults. An overload was the alternative and is worse: two candidates PostgREST can resolve to, against "one place decides each thing". It also refuses to change `third_place` once a draw exists, because the draw reads it at generation and changing it afterwards would silently do nothing.
- **The draw settings live on this page, not the create page**, because generation is the last moment they mean anything. The empty state is the setup: who goes in (confirmed only or everyone not withdrawn, with a live count of each), progression, match creation, third place — then Generate.
- `tdc_resolve_draw` is called on load and after every write. Calling it on load is why a TD who scored three matches on somebody else's phone opens this page to a bracket that is already up to date.
- **Rounds are columns and the page scrolls sideways.** No connector lines: at phone width they cost more than they explain, and each fixture carries its number (`3-2`) while the next column says "Winner of 2-1", which is the same information in less ink. It prints, because a draw sheet on the wall is still how most of a club night is read.
- An unreached slot says where its player will come from rather than sitting blank — a draw sheet is readable before it is played, and that is most of what it is for.
- The redraw button does not write its own warning. It calls `tdc_generate_draw`, and if the database refuses with the already-started message, **that message is what the person is asked to confirm** — so the warning they read is the real count rather than a guess maintained in two places.
- Rounds are named from the end (Final, Semi-finals, Quarter-finals, then "Round n"), so the naming survives any field size.

### Overrides and officials (migration `20261020100000`)
Built to `claude/approval-and-corrections.md`, which is the design record for all of this.
- **`private.tdc_can_edit_match` now knows competition roles exist.** It never did, so a tournament director who was neither a player in the match nor staff at the venue could not touch their own tournament's matches — principle 8 was false in the database. One extra branch in the one function, per principle 4, joining through `tbl_tournament_fixtures` because a match belongs to a competition through its fixture and not through a column.
- **`tdc_add_competition_role` / `tdc_remove_competition_role` / `tdc_competition_officials`.** Until now the only insert into `tbl_competition_roles` anywhere was the director created inside `tdc_create_tournament`, so referees and scorers could not be appointed at all. Appointing is gated on `tdc_can_run_tournament`, so a scorer (rank 30) cannot appoint anybody — which is what the ranks are for. Removing the only director of a venue-less tournament is refused: nobody would ever be able to run it again.
- **`tbl_match_result_override`** is the design note's table, unchanged: one per match, `reason` mandatory, `counted_home` / `counted_away` recording what the frames said at the time, and `card_version` recording what it was set against.
  - **Reading a score is one branch in one place** — `private.tdc_match_score`: the override if there is one, otherwise count the frames. Everything reads through it.
  - **Only somebody running the competition the match belongs to.** A social match has no coordinator and therefore no override, which is right: nobody's word outranks the two people who played it.
  - **Corrected 20 Oct, migration `20261020110000`:** the original refused an override on a match whose status is `in_progress`. That read the note's open question 5 too literally — `in_progress` does not mean people are at the table, it means frames exist and the card has not been submitted, which is exactly the state a TD-run match sits in. Combined with `tdc_submit_card` accepting only the players in a match, it left a TD able to record frames, unable to submit, and unable to override: a dead end in the main flow. The refusal is gone; the page asks instead, per the house rule of confirm-and-repair rather than block. A reason and coordinator rights are still required.
  - **A later frame edit makes an override stale, never drops it.** A coordinator overriding a score is deliberately overruling the frames, so the page flags it for review. *(Same.)*
  - Clearing it restores the counted score, which a score written onto the match could never do.
- `tdc_fixture_outcome` and `tdc_tournament_draw` both read through `tdc_match_score`, so a TD's score advances the bracket and the draw shows it labelled as set by hand rather than disguised as a result.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres: a TD who is neither player nor venue staff can edit, a stranger cannot, a referee can edit but can neither appoint nor override; an override beats the frames and advances the bracket from them, keeps what the frames said, goes stale on a card change without being dropped, and is reversible; reasonless, mid-match and non-coordinator overrides are all refused.
- **Known, not yet fixed — a TD cannot finish a card.** `tdc_submit_card` requires `private.tdc_my_player_on_match`, so only a player in the match may submit. In the core case — the TD runs it and neither player uses the app — the TD can record frames but cannot submit, so the match never reaches `complete`. Found by audit on 20 Oct; predates the tournament work but blocks it. The override is the way through for now. The fix belongs with the publishing work below, because who may finish a card and who may publish it are the same question.
- **Not here yet:** coordinator approval itself (`coordinator_approval` and `both` still behave like a dispute window), the role-rank conflict rule on frame writes (a lower rank overwriting a higher rank's frame), any page for officials or overrides, the public read-only view, and double elimination.

### Publishing — the shape UV described, 20 Oct
Not built. Recorded here so it is not re-derived.
- The bracket must advance on **published**, not recorded. It currently advances on `status = 'complete'`, which for a dispute window happens at submission — 48 hours early. Found by audit 20 Oct.
- UV's three cases map onto settings that already exist rather than anything new:

| Case | Settings | Published when |
|---|---|---|
| Player results publish immediately | `approval_mode: none` + `score_entry_mode: players` | On submission; the card locks |
| Only the TD publishes | `approval_mode: coordinator_approval` + `players` | On the TD's approve, or his override-and-publish |
| TD enters everything | `score_entry_mode: td_only` + override | On the override |

- So publishing is `approval_mode` finally meaning what it says, plus an `approved` card event retired by `card_version` like a submission — not a new concept.
- **An override by the TD publishes**, because the TD is the approver; overriding is implicitly approving.
- `score_entry_mode` is validated but **enforced nowhere**. `td_only` currently restricts nothing.
- **Answered 20 Oct (UV), and built — migration `20261020120000`:**
  - **`approval_mode` and `dispute_window_hours` are two independent axes in a competition.** The mode decides *when it publishes* (`none` / `dispute_window` on submission; `coordinator_approval` / `both` on the coordinator's approval). The hours decide *how long a player may object afterwards*. This corrects the design note's guess about `both`, which assumed the coordinator could not approve until the players had settled.
  - **Publishing locks the card. A dispute never reopens it and never un-publishes it** — the objection is recorded and goes to the coordinator, who lets it stand or changes the score.
  - **An override publishes.** The coordinator is the approver; making them press a second button would only produce matches that are overridden and not published.
  - **A coordinator may correct a locked card**, through the lock guard rather than a session flag. Nobody else may.
  - **Publication exists only inside a competition.** A social match has no bracket or standings to publish to, so `dispute_window` keeps exactly the meaning it has today. *This was caught by the social regression suite after a first version published and locked every social `dispute_window` match on submission, leaving the opponent unable to agree or dispute.*
  - `tdc_submit_card` now accepts anyone `tdc_can_edit_match` permits, which closes the audit finding that a TD could record every frame of a match between two walk-ins and then had no way to say the card was done.
  - New: `tdc_approve_card`, `tdc_reject_card` (reason required, for the same reason a dispute needs one), `private.tdc_published_at`, `private.tdc_is_published`, `private.tdc_card_is_locked`, `private.tdc_match_competition`. `tdc_fixture_outcome` advances on **published**, not on `status = 'complete'`.
  - `match.js` gained the `approved` state, the sent-back note and the objection note. Without it an approved card rendered an empty card panel and the page showed the scoring stage, because `settled()` did not know the state existed.
  - The bracket page gained Publish and Send back, plus a badge saying whether a result counts yet.
- **Corrected 20 Oct after UV's first real run — migration `20261020140000`:**
  - **A published card reported state `awaiting`**, so the match page offered "Looks right" and the database then refused it: the card was published and locked at submission. `tdc_card_state` now reports a published card as settled and never as waiting, and carries `can_object` / `object_until`. Once a result is published there is nothing to agree to — only something to object to, and only while the window is open. The match page offers exactly that and no agree button.
  - **`tdc_approve_card` refused every mode except `coordinator_approval`**, on the reasoning that the others publish by themselves so there is nothing to approve. There is: ending it. Approving now works in every mode — it publishes one that was waiting, locks a settled `none` card against reopening, and ends a `dispute_window` early. In all three the match becomes published, locked and terminal with no further player input. *(UV: the TD should be able to mark any completed match published and locked.)*
  - The bracket's badge reads **published first** and the card's own state only while unpublished, because published is the only thing that decides whether the draw moves. "Not agreed yet" on a result that was already counting was the confusing half of what UV saw.
### Who may enter scores (migration `20261021100000`)
- **`score_entry_mode` finally means something.** It has existed since `20261015100000`, is validated, and is offered on the create page with four values — and until 21 Oct it was enforced nowhere, so a tournament set to `td_only` let any player in the match score it. A setting that silently does nothing is worse than no setting, because the TD picks it and believes it.
- **Enforced inside `private.tdc_can_edit_match`, not in a new trigger.** Principle 4: that function is already the one place deciding whether a card may be touched, and the match page already asks it through `tdc_may_edit_match` before drawing any controls. Putting it there means the page stops *offering* scoring buttons to somebody who may not score, rather than offering them and having the database refuse — the house rule about not offering buttons that lead to invalid states.
- The ranks are the existing ones: `players` 0, `scorer` 30, `referee` 50, `td_only` 100. `private.tdc_score_rank` gives whoever runs the competition 100, so venue staff are covered wherever a tournament belongs to a venue.
- **A social match is untouched.** It has no competition and no officials, so a ruleset key about who may score has no meaning when the only people involved are the two playing. Verified: a social match carrying `td_only` in its ruleset still lets its players score.
- `tdc_score_entry_note` gives the page a sentence to show when the controls are missing for this reason, so a player sees "Only the tournament director enters scores in this tournament" rather than a blank card. It says nothing to a stranger to the match, because telling them about a tournament they are not in is noise.
- **Known rough edge:** if a write is attempted anyway (through the API rather than the page), `tdc_record_frame`'s existing refusal reads *"You are not on this match and cannot change its score"*, which is wrong for a player who is on it but outranked. Rewriting that 200-line function for one string was not worth the regression risk; the page never reaches it.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres across all four modes with a player, a scorer, a referee and the TD: the permission matrix is exact, the refusal is real on a write and not just advertised, and all four existing suites still pass.
- **Still not built:** the role-rank conflict rule on overwrites — a lower rank overwriting a frame a higher rank already touched. That needs `tbl_frame_edit`, the audit log, which does not exist yet.

### Tournament status (migration `20261021120000`)
- **It never changed.** The column has existed since `20261019140000` with five values and a check constraint, and nothing ever wrote it, so every tournament read "Draft" forever — including the badge on the list, which is the first thing you see. A column that is always the same value is worse than none, because the badge is believed.
- **Derived, not synced.** `draft` with nobody entered, `open` with entrants and no draw, `in_progress` with a draw, `complete` once the final's outcome is decided. All facts the database already holds, so the status is counted rather than stored — the same rule that keeps scores out of `tbl_matches`. The alternative was writing the column from `tdc_add_entrant`, `tdc_remove_entrant`, `tdc_generate_draw`, `tdc_clear_draw`, `tdc_resolve_draw` and whatever comes next: six places to keep in step and a seventh somebody forgets, which is how a status column goes stale in the first place.
- **Except when the TD says otherwise.** A derivation cannot express "cancelled", or a TD closing entries early. So the stored column stays as the override, with `status_manual` saying it is in force — **the same shape as `tbl_frames.break_side_manual`**: setting it by hand raises the flag, the derivation leaves it alone from then on, and there is a way back to the rule. A TD who has met one already understands the other.
- Read it through `private.tdc_tournament_status`, never off the column. `public.tdc_tournament_status` also returns what the derivation *would* say, so the page can offer "follow the tournament again" as a known quantity rather than something you find out by pressing it.
- The list shows a **"Set by hand"** badge beside a manual status: a cancelled tournament should not look like something the system worked out.
- **Found while doing it:** the format guard in `tdc_update_tournament` tested `status <> 'draft'` and had therefore *never fired once*, because the status was always `'draft'` — the format of a tournament with a full draw could always be changed. It now tests the thing it was always about, whether a draw exists.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres through the whole lifecycle: draft → open → in_progress → complete, cancelled by hand with the derivation reporting it would say "complete", handed back and returning to complete, and the format guard firing and then allowing the change once the draw was cleared. All eight earlier suites still pass.

### Entering a tournament (migration `20261021130000`)
- **Until now the only way into a tournament was a TD typing your name.** Every entrant write was gated on running the tournament. UV's flow, 21 Oct: the TD creates a tournament and decides how people get in, shares a link, a player marks themselves interested, and the TD confirms them on whatever criterion they use — often payment. The ladder itself (`interested` → `confirmed`) was settled long ago; what is new is a player reaching the first rung.
- **`entry_mode` on `tbl_tournaments`**, three values. `td_only` (the default, so every tournament that already exists keeps behaving exactly as it did), `interested` — anyone may put themselves down and the TD confirms — and `self_confirm`, for a free tournament or one paid for at the desk.
- **`paid_confirms`, off by default.** UV: "we should add an option on tournament creation where (optionally) paid can trigger confirmed". A TD who confirms on something other than payment would be surprised by it, so it is a choice. Folded into `tdc_set_entrant`, where **an explicit `p_status` still wins** — a TD saying "paid, but withdrawn" means it.
- **Entering never lowers you.** `tdc_enter_tournament` upserts, and the naive version took the new value — so a player pressing "interested" again would undo a confirmation the TD had already given. It now raises to `confirmed` where the mode permits that and otherwise brings a withdrawal back to `interested`. Withdrawing and coming back is the case this had to keep working.
- **A verified account is `auth.users.email_confirmed_at`.** UV: self-entry needs a verified account but not a verified ID. That is the only half of "verified" the database can check; the other half is the signup bot check, which leaves no trace here and **is still switched off** — so this is the floor, not the whole of the intent, and the bot check stays on UV's list.
- **Withdrawing sets a status, it does not delete.** The TD needs to see that somebody pulled out, and a seeded draw that silently loses a name is worse than one with a withdrawal in it. Withdrawn entrants still do not hold a place against `max_entrants`, matching `tdc_add_entrant`.

### The poster (migration `20261021140000`)
- **The only page on this site a stranger can read**, and the only function granted to `anon`. UV's design, carried from the earlier chat: a TD sets a tournament up to the point where they can copy a block of text describing the whole thing, hand it to an AI and get a custom poster back. It works before, during or after — during and after including progression.
- **It is a snapshot, not a public read of the live tables.** The obvious build was to grant `anon` a narrow read of `tbl_tournaments` and a cut-down draw. **UV, 21 Oct: "maybe you route the information through the TD's account rather than letting unsigned accounts access the DB?"** — which is the better design, for three reasons:
  1. Every `tdc_` function written so far assumes a caller with a player record. Opening any of them to `anon` means re-auditing each one for a caller who has none, and the cost of missing one is a stranger reading walk-in records — people who never chose to be findable — or a TD's private entrant notes.
  2. A poster *is* a snapshot. It is a thing the TD produces at a moment, not a live window. Generating it is already the gesture.
  3. It collapses the exposure question to one reviewable place: `tbl_tournament_posters.snapshot`. **Anything not in that blob is not exposed**, so there is one place to look when asking what a stranger can read.
- **The cost, stated plainly:** a published poster goes stale. It shows the tournament as it was when generated. That is correct for a poster and wrong for a scoreboard, so the page says when it was made and the TD regenerates. **A live public scoreboard is a different feature** and would need the `anon` read this deliberately avoids.
- **Generating and sharing are separate acts.** `tdc_publish_poster` builds the snapshot; `is_public` is what makes it readable, and defaults to false. A TD can build a poster to read the text out of it without making anything public. Passing `p_public => null` leaves the setting alone, so **regenerating never changes whether it is shared** — in either direction.
- **What is in it by default:** the tournament, and entrant *counts*. UV: "the poster page is just the tournament information and maybe how many confirmed/interested entrants there are... the actual tournament information with final entrants and history etc. is for the TD only." Entrant names and the draw are **opt-in per generation**. (UV's two answers pointed different ways on this — the other was "everything the draw shows" — and the snapshot design dissolves the conflict into a setting, which is also the pattern everywhere else here: the TD decides.)
- **What is deliberately absent:** `notes` (the TD's), every id but the tournament's own, and `score_entry_mode` / `approval_mode` / `dispute_window_hours` — how a result is agreed is between the TD and the players, not poster copy.
- **`private.tdc_poster_rules` is a placeholder for `player_facing`.** The decision was that the setup page, the round settings box, the per-match override dropdown and this text are all renderings of one definition in `TDCMatchDef`, driven by a `player_facing` flag. **That flag still does not exist**, so the list of player-facing keys lives in this function and this is its only caller. **When the flag lands, delete this function.**
- A result that has not been published is carried as `published: false` and the page marks it "not confirmed yet" — a poster that showed it as settled would make a claim the TD has not. A hand-set score is shown as one, the same as on the draw.
- **A poster holds display names, not player ids**, so `tdc_merge_players` has nothing to repoint — but a snapshot built before a merge shows the old name until regenerated. Recorded in a column comment rather than fixed: rewriting names inside a stored blob is the kind of cleverness that goes wrong, and the poster is explicitly a snapshot of a moment.
- `tdc_tournament_poster` serves two callers from one function: a stranger or ordinary player gets a shared poster, and whoever runs the tournament also gets an unshared one so they can see what they are about to share. **A missing poster and a private one return the same nothing** — saying which would tell a stranger the tournament exists.
- `tournaments/poster/index.html` + `scripts/tournament-poster.js` + `stylesheets/poster.css`. **The only page using `TDC.getSession` rather than `TDC.requireSession`** — a signed-out visitor is the expected case and must not be bounced to the login screen. Rounds run *down* the page, not sideways as on the draw: the draw is a working document read at the desk, this is read once on a phone by somebody deciding whether to come. Labels come from `TDCMatchDef.SETTINGS`, so the poster and the settings panel cannot call the same setting different things; a key with no definition is reported rather than dropped.
- Entry buttons live here, driven by `tdc_my_entry`. A signed-out visitor is shown "Sign in to enter" rather than nothing — **attracting somebody who has not signed up is the point of the page**. Where the mode allows self-confirmation the weaker "I'm interested" is still offered: deciding to come is not the same as committing.
- **Found while doing it — not a hole, but one grant away from being one.** 14 `private.tdc_*` functions (trigger functions and pure helpers) have no `revoke ... from public`, so `anon` shows as having EXECUTE on them. It cannot reach them: `anon` has no `usage` on schema `private`. So the schema grant is the only thing standing between those 14 and an unauthenticated caller. **Worth its own small hardening migration** — deliberately not folded into this one, since a permissions sweep is a separate concern from the poster.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres, including the part that matters: `anon` can execute exactly one `tdc_` function and select exactly one table, sees zero rows of an unshared poster and one of a shared one, and is refused `update` and `delete` outright. Plus generating-is-not-sharing both ways, regeneration preserving the sharing state in both directions, the player-facing filter holding, counts moving only on regeneration, and a non-TD refused both verbs. All ten earlier suites still pass.

### Swapping a player in a fixture (migration `20261021150000`)
- **UV, 21 Oct:** *"Basically on every match, TD should be able to swap out a player. I think we should warn and cascade... it will serve more function at the start of a tournament where TD can randomize the player list and then auto-populate the first round and then make edits."* Two uses from one control: editing an auto-populated first round before play, where nothing is at stake, and correcting a running tournament, where a great deal is.
- **Why not just `tdc_set_fixture`.** That already sets a slot and already detaches it from its feeder. What it does not do is notice the match. **`tbl_frames` records a winner as a *side*; `tbl_matches` names a *player*.** So changing a fixture's entrant without touching its match leaves the two disagreeing — the bracket reads Cal v Bob while the match behind it is still Ann v Bob, and `tdc_fixture_outcome` reads the match. Repointing the match at Cal instead would silently turn two frames Ann played into Cal's record.
- **The rule was already decided, in the cascade.** `20261020130000`: *"The match in this fixture was between two particular people. If either of them is changing, it is no longer this fixture's match. It keeps its frames and stops belonging to the draw."* The swap follows that rather than inventing a second answer to one question. Nothing is destroyed — the FK runs fixture → match, so the played match survives and simply stops being part of a bracket.
- **Rehearse, then apply**, same shape and same subtransaction trick as `tdc_cascade_draw`. `p_apply` defaults to false, so the first call is always a question, and what the TD is asked to confirm is what will actually happen rather than the page's guess. The cascade is **called, not reimplemented** — it is the one place that knows how a change propagates, and it is correctly a no-op under manual progression.
- **A reason is required once frames exist.** Re-attributing somebody's night should not be a two-tap action with no note of who did it or why; it goes onto the fixture's notes. Before any frames are played, no reason is asked for — the first-round-editing case stays frictionless.
- **A published result refuses the swap** and names the way through. Changing the draw under an agreed result would alter a published fact.
- **An entrant already elsewhere in the draw is reported, not refused.** A TD rearranging a bracket by hand legitimately passes through states where somebody is in two places — the same reason `tdc_set_fixture` refuses only the finished article being wrong — so refusing would force the swaps into an order the TD had to work out. But `also_in` carries it, with `same_round` flagged, because two fixtures in one round holding the same player is a bracket nobody can play.
- **`tdc_fixture_candidates`** is the narrow read for the picker. The entrant list is gated and carries payment status and private notes; a dropdown needs names, statuses and where somebody already is. Withdrawn entrants are still offered — a TD correcting a withdrawal entered by mistake has to be able to put them back — with the status returned so the page says what it is.
- **A picker, not drag-and-drop.** UV asked for dragging a player from the confirmed list onto a match. Same idea, different gesture: a TD at the desk is on a phone, and dragging across a sideways-scrolling bracket is a mis-tap waiting to happen. The function serves either, so it can become a drag later without schema change.
- The open picker suppresses the live re-read, exactly as a half-typed score already did: it holds a chosen name and a typed reason.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres: the rehearsal changing nothing and the match keeping its frames through it; applying; emptying a slot and filling it again; a stranger's id, a bad side and somebody opposite themselves all refused; a no-op reported as one; the reason requirement firing and then being satisfied; the detached match surviving with its two frames and its original two players; a published result refused; and **a walkover-advanced entrant swapped out, with the award cleared and the final losing them in a second cascade pass**.

### Rejecting a card actually rejects it (migration `20261021160000`)
- **A bug found while building the swap, which needed a way to unlock a published result and named "reject or override" as the two routes. Only one of them worked.**
- `tdc_approve_card` writes an `approved` event; `tdc_reject_card` writes a `rejected` one and sets the match back to `in_progress`. But `private.tdc_card_state` looked for the latest `approved` event at the current `card_version` and returned `approved` the moment it found one, **before ever looking at what happened afterwards**. `private.tdc_published_at` did the same. So after approving and then rejecting: `state=approved`, `published=true`, `locked=true`. **The "send it back" button did nothing but write a note** — no way back from a mistaken approval, frames still locked, and the bracket still advancing on the result.
- **Why it was written that way, and why that reasoning was right.** The comment reads *"An approval of the card as it stands is terminal. A later objection is recorded against it but does not undo it."* That is correct and deliberate — a **player objecting** after publication must not unpublish a result, which is the whole point of `20261020140000`. The mistake is that the branch did not distinguish an objection from a rejection. They are different acts by different people: `disputed` is a player saying the card is wrong (recorded, flagged, does not unlock — **unchanged**); `rejected` is the coordinator withdrawing their *own* approval, which must take effect.
- **The fix is one `not exists` clause in three places** — an approval counts only while no `rejected` event of the same `card_version` is newer than it. `tdc_published_at`, `tdc_card_state`, and `tdc_approve_card`'s own guard.
- **The third place mattered.** Fixing the first two alone produced a card that could be sent back and then **never approved again**, because `tdc_approve_card` (as replaced in `20261020140000`) refuses when an `approved` event exists at the current version. That is a worse dead end than the one being fixed, and it was only caught by testing the full approve → reject → re-approve cycle rather than stopping at "reject now works".
- **Caught myself retyping.** My first draft of `tdc_card_state` was written out by hand and silently invented a different tail: it defaulted `dispute_window_hours` to 0 instead of 48, dropped settling-by-silence, dropped the published-plus-disputed → `agreed` branch, and returned `in_progress` where the original returns `awaiting`. That would have broken the whole dispute flow. **Caught by diffing against the original with comments stripped** — the same mistake and the same catch as the `tdc_merge_players` rewrite. Both functions here are the original patched by script, with the diff verified to be only the new clause. *If you are ever replacing one of these, patch the text; do not retype it.*
- Double approval is still refused, and all eleven other suites pass unchanged.
- **Corrected 23 Oct, migration `20261023185945` — that "verified" diff was not only the new clause.** The pgTAP suites found that `20261021160000` had also dropped `can_object` / `object_until` and moved "published means settled" into the disputed branch, so the 20 Oct bug was live again: a card published on submission read `awaiting`, the other player was offered "Looks right" (refused), and the objection button could never appear. The likely reason for the move: in `none` / `dispute_window` a send-back did not unpublish (published came from the submission alone), so with the branch in place a sent-back card would have read as settled.
  - **A send-back now really unlocks a card published on submission** — `tdc_published_at` ignores a submission with a newer `rejected` event of the same version, the clause approvals already had. UV, 23 Oct (`docs/DECISIONS.md`): a settled card is final for players; the TD or LD can open it again.
  - With that, the published branch and the two fields went back exactly as `20261020140000` wrote them. Proven by `014_match_approval`.
  - **Not changed, worth knowing:** a send-back unpublishes the result, so the bracket stops treating it as decided — exactly as a send-back of an approved card already did since 21 Oct. Whether a slot the result had already filled in the next round is emptied again is the progression code's business; not checked here.

### The frame edit log (migration `20261021110000`)
- **`tbl_frame_edit`** — every change to every frame: who, when, their rank at the time, the card version it produced, and the whole row before and after as jsonb. Written by an **after trigger**, not by the write functions: `tdc_record_frame` and `tdc_remove_frame` are not the only paths (a coordinator correcting a locked card, the merge, anything added later), and a log with a hole in it is worse than no log because it is believed.
- `before` / `after` are jsonb rather than a column per field, so adding a column to `tbl_frames` cannot silently stop being logged.
- **`editor_role` and `editor_rank` are frozen at the time of the edit.** Roles change — a scorer in October may be the director in March — and resolving the role at read time would rewrite history every time somebody was promoted.
- **`created_at` defaults to `clock_timestamp()`, not `now()`.** Same correction `20261015170000` made to `tbl_match_events`, for the same reason: `now()` is transaction time, so several frames written in one transaction share a timestamp and the history comes back jumbled. Caught here by a test that wrote four edits in one block and got them back out of order.
- **The rank rule, finally.** `private.tdc_frame_rank_guard` refuses a frame write when somebody senior has already touched that frame, unless the writer is at least their equal. Equal ranks may overwrite each other, which is what lets two players score their own match. Outside a competition everyone is rank 0, so it never fires on social play. The refusal names who to ask: *"Frame 1 was last set by the referee. Ask them to change it."*
  - **Since 24 Oct (`20261024162559`) a match's own referee is rank 50 too**, in any match, and the guard also fires on a non-tournament match that has one. A league director is rank 100 on their league's matches. See `docs/systems/matches.md`, **A match's own referee**.
- `tdc_frame_history(match_id, since_version)` returns what changed in words — `winner_side home → away`, by name and role — rather than two blobs to diff by eye. It reports only the fields a person would recognise; timings and the clicker log change constantly and would bury the one line somebody is looking for.
- **`tdc_merge_players` was four tables out of date, and it was losing data.** `CLAUDE.md` carries the warning to add new tables to its move section; four had been added since it was last touched and none of them were — `tbl_tournament_entrants.player_id` and `tbl_competition_roles.player_id` (both cascade), `tbl_match_result_override.set_by` and the new `tbl_frame_edit.edited_by_player_id` (both set null).
  - **Verified against the old function, not assumed:** a signup record holding one tournament entry, one official role, one frame edit and one override was merged, and the kept record came out with **none of them**. The entry and the role were hard-deleted by the cascade; the other two survived with the person forgotten. That is the "loses its history quietly" failure the original comment warns about, on the one flow flagged as able to lose real data.
  - The fixed version was built by **patching the original verbatim**, not retyping it: a first attempt rewritten by hand had silently dropped two guards (walk-in-only, and the played-against-each-other check). The diff against the original is additions only.
  - The frame updates now run inside `tdc.card_unlock`, because they pass the new rank guard as well as the lock. A merge is bookkeeping, not somebody changing a score.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres: social play unblocked and fully logged; in a competition a player scores, a referee corrects, the player is refused by name, the player can still score an untouched frame, and the TD outranks the referee; the merge moves all four tables with nothing orphaned; all five earlier suites still pass.
- **Not built:** notifying a player whose frame was changed by somebody else (the design asks for it), and any page showing the history — `tdc_frame_history` has no caller yet.

### Cascade (migration `20261020130000`)
UV, 20 Oct: with `progression: automatic` a change upstream cascades downstream with warnings; with `manual` the TD changes each affected match by hand.
- **`tdc_resolve_draw` cannot do this and deliberately still cannot.** It only ever fills an empty slot, which is what makes it safe to call on every page load — it can add somebody to the draw but never take them out. Undoing is a different act, so it is a different function.
- **Drift** is a slot fed by `winner_of` / `loser_of` that does not hold whoever that fixture's outcome names. A slot the TD placed by hand is sourced `entrant` and is never drift, which is the same rule that makes `tdc_set_fixture` stick.
- **`tdc_cascade_draw(tournament, p_apply)` with `p_apply` false is a real rehearsal, not an estimate.** The work runs inside a block with an exception handler — which gives it a subtransaction — and is then deliberately aborted. Every database change rolls back; the report is a plpgsql variable, which is not transactional, so it survives and is returned. The warning the TD reads is therefore exactly what will happen, three rounds of knock-on included, rather than a guess at the first layer.
- The loop is bounded at ten passes. A bracket is finite and each pass settles at least one round; the bound exists so a wiring mistake cannot spin forever.
- **A match between what are now the wrong two people is detached, not deleted.** Those frames were really played. The match survives on its own and the fixture goes back to needing one; deleting is `tdc_delete_match`, asked for deliberately. A walkover awarded to somebody no longer in the fixture is cleared.
- With `progression: manual` it does nothing and says so — cascading would be the system overruling the TD, which is the opposite of what that setting is for.
- The bracket page shows the rehearsal as a panel listing each change, names how many already-played matches would come out of the draw, and only then offers the button.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres with a fully played 8-player bracket: overriding fixture 1-1 the other way, the rehearsal reported the semi-final and final changes and two published matches to detach, and left the database untouched; applying it reseated the semi, emptied the final, kept both detached matches with their frames (7 matches before, 8 after — one new semi, nothing deleted), left a hand-placed entrant alone, and did nothing at all under manual progression.


### A round's own rules (migration `20261022140000`)
A tournament's ruleset is its default; a round may differ — race to 3 early, race to 5 from the semis. UV, 22 Oct: stored and editable before the round starts, applied to matches that already exist **after showing which ones**, with a checkbox for "and later rounds".
- **This does not break the frozen-ruleset principle, it completes it.** A match copies its ruleset at creation so that a later change cannot rescore played frames. That frozen copy says what a match *was played under*. The round row says what a match *started from now on* will use. Before this, a match created later in a round silently got the tournament's rules, which is the thing that was actually wrong.
- **`tbl_tournament_round_rules`**, keyed `(tournament_id, bracket, round)`. **No row means the round follows the tournament** — nothing to create up front, nothing to backfill, and clearing is a delete rather than a flag.
- **`private.tdc_rules_for_round(tournament, bracket, round)` is the one place that resolves it**, round first then tournament. `tdc_create_fixture_match` calls it instead of reading `tournament.rules`, which is the single line that makes the whole thing work.
- **`tdc_set_round_rules` rehearses before it writes**, the same subtransaction pattern as the cascade: `p_apply` defaults to false, and the first call returns which matches change, how many are **being played right now**, and which are **skipped and why**. The TD confirms against the report, not against a guess.
- **A card that is agreed or published is skipped, not fought with.** It is reported as skipped with its reason.
- **A match being played keeps its frames** and has its break order derived again under the new rules. Nothing is rescored — the derivation was always a function of the frames plus the ruleset.
- **A ruleset the matches would refuse is refused here too**, by the same validation. There is no second, looser check.
- `p_and_later` covers every round above this one that is already drawn. `tdc_clear_round_rules` takes it too.
- `public.tdc_round_rules(tournament)` lists the stored rows with a match count, for the page.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres (`/var/tmp/tests/roundrules.sql`): a round with no row inherits; setting the final to race 5 before anyone reaches it leaves round 1 alone and freezes 5 onto the final when it is created; the rehearsal on a round with two live matches reported them and changed nothing; applying re-derived them and kept their frames; an agreed card was skipped; `and later` covered every drawn round; a bad ruleset was refused; clearing handed the round back while already-played matches kept what they were played under; and a player could neither set nor clear.

- It is built from `TDCMatchDef`, not from a hand-written form. `control(def, rules, prefix)`, `liveRuleKeys(rules)` and `readValue(def, raw)` were added to the module for this; the `prefix` argument exists so two settings panels on one page cannot collide on element ids. **The create page still builds its own older copy of the same form** — recorded as a gap, not fixed here.
- The confirmation text is built line by line out of the rehearsal. Nothing in it is worked out in the browser.
- **`.tdc-card button` in `forms.css` is a full-width pill and outranks a single class.** The heading pill came out 233×45px, stretched to the whole column, until it was written as `.tdc-card .bracket-round-rules` with `width`, `height` and `margin` undone explicitly. Found by rendering it in headless Chromium — nothing in the markup or the stylesheet reads as wrong.

### Editing a draw that is already running (migration `20261022150000`)
UV, 22 Oct: a 16-man tournament starts, two players arrive late, and the TD wants them in without recreating anything — add a match to a round, then say where its winner goes.
- **No structural change was needed.** `tbl_tournament_fixtures` already stores the source **per slot**, not per pairing: `home_source_type`/`ref` and `away_source_type`/`ref` are independent. So `R1M1 & R1M2 → R2M1` becoming `R1M1 & R1M3 → R2M1` is one slot changing its `away_source_ref`. Nothing is resorted and nothing downstream is regenerated.
- **`tdc_set_fixture_source(fixture, side, source_type, source_ref, p_apply, reason)`** repoints one slot at `winner_of` / `loser_of` another fixture, at an `entrant` directly, or at nothing. Rehearses by default, same subtransaction pattern as the cascade.
- **Pointing a slot at a fixture empties it.** The slot is being told to wait for a result, and `tdc_resolve_draw` only ever fills an *empty* slot — leaving the old occupant there would mean the new wire could never fire and the TD would be looking at an arrow that does nothing. Who was displaced is in the report.
- **A bye is cleared when its empty slot gets a source.** A bye means "nobody can ever be here, so the other side goes through"; giving the slot a source makes that untrue. This is exactly the late-arrivals case on a draw with byes.
- **Loops are refused**, by walking upward from the proposed source (`private.tdc_fixture_would_loop`, bounded at 64 so an already-cyclic graph cannot spin). Pointing R1M1 at the winner of R2M1 is refused with the reason, not a constraint violation.
- **A fixture with a match is refused outright.** Its sides are settled and the frames are the record; detaching stays a deliberate separate act.
- **`tdc_add_fixture(tournament, round, bracket, home, away, notes)`** appends a match at the next free position in a round that already exists. **No rehearsal** — nothing can point at a fixture that did not exist a moment ago, so nothing downstream can change. It returns `needs_wiring: true` so the page prompts for the arrow instead of leaving a match whose winner goes nowhere.
- **Only into an existing round.** Adding past the last round would be round insertion through the back door, and that has to renumber `tbl_tournament_round_rules`.
- **`tdc_fixture_source_candidates(fixture)`** is the narrow read for the picker: everything in the tournament that is not this fixture and would not make a loop, with labels and whether each is decided yet.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres (`/var/tmp/tests/editdraw.sql`): UV's scenario end to end — a 4-player draw, two late entrants, `tdc_add_fixture` making R1M3, the rehearsal reporting the rewire and changing nothing, applying it, and then **R1M3 being played and advancing Eve into the slot that was rewired** while R1M2's winner feeds nothing. Plus eight refusals (loop, self, bad side, bad source type, dangling `winner_of`, a fixture with a match, a round outside the draw, somebody against themselves), all three functions refused to a player, and a bye cleared by seating a late arrival in its empty slot.
- **Not here:** inserting a round, and the arrows on the page. See `docs/STATUS.md`.

### Inserting a round (migration `20261022160000`)
UV, 22 Oct: a "+" on the border between two rounds. A prelim in front of round 1 is the case that matters. UV chose **renumbering** over sparse ordering — one awkward function rather than a second concept that never goes away.
- **Exactly two tables store a round number**, which is what made renumbering the cheaper side of the trade: `tbl_tournament_fixtures` and `tbl_tournament_round_rules`. Everything else that mentions a round computes it per call.
- **There is no rounds table and this does not add one.** A round exists because fixtures are in it, so inserting a round is *only* the renumber: it leaves a **gap** in the sequence. The page draws `1..max` rather than the distinct rounds that have fixtures, and the gap is the empty round. The arrows pass over it on their own, because the round above still points at the round below.
- **`private.tdc_shift_rounds` moves rounds in two passes, on purpose.** The unique constraint on `(tournament_id, bracket, round, position)` is checked per row, so a single `set round = round + 1` fails the moment it moves round 1 onto round 2 while round 2 is still there. Making the constraint deferrable would push the failure out to `COMMIT` — past the rehearsal's subtransaction, and therefore past where it can be caught and reported. So the affected rows move clear of every existing number first, then come back one higher. Every check stays immediate.
- **`tdc_insert_round(tournament, before_round, bracket, p_apply)`** rehearses and reports how many fixtures are renumbered, how many stored rulesets move with them, and how many matches are **being played in the rounds that shift** — which are unaffected, but a TD watching a round change number mid-match deserves to be told that.
- **`tdc_delete_round`** closes an empty round back up. Refuses while it still has fixtures: removing a match is a separate deliberate act, not a side effect of a layout change.
- **`tdc_remove_fixture`** is the undo for `tdc_add_fixture`. Refuses while it has a match (detaching keeps the frames; removing would throw them away) and while anything is still wired to it — naming every slot that is, because the source refs have no foreign key and nothing else would catch a dangling wire.
- **`tdc_add_fixture` now allows one round past the last**, which makes a new round on top and moves nothing. The previous refusal was wider than its reason. Further than one past would leave gaps nobody asked for, and anywhere below the top is `tdc_insert_round`'s job.
- Verified on a throwaway Postgres (`/var/tmp/tests/insertround.sql`): **a race-to-5 ruleset on the final followed it from round 2 to round 3 on insert and home again on delete** — the whole reason renumbering costs anything; a match being played came through with **the same frozen ruleset and the same frames, only a new number**; the rehearsal changed nothing; the gap took a new match and wired into the round above; a round added on the end moved nothing; and all four refusals fired, with all three functions refused to a player.
- **Still derived, still wrong at the edges:** `roundName()` works out "Semi-finals" from distance to the last round, which stops being true once a prelim is in front of it. Fixing it by counting fixtures in the round instead (1 = Final, 2 = Semi-finals, 4 = Quarter-finals) is noted with the arrows work.

### The progression arrows (migration `20261022170000`, `tournament-bracket.js`, `bracket.css`)
UV, 22 Oct: "progression arrows between each round … the arrows should be smart so that if TD swaps players as they progress from one round to the next, then the arrows will show that change rather than resorting the entire tournament."
- **They are smart for free, and that is the point.** Each arrow is drawn straight from a slot's `home_feeder_id` / `away_feeder_id` — the same column `tdc_resolve_draw` reads when it fills that slot. There is no second model of progression in the page to fall out of step with the first. Repoint a slot and the arrow moves, because **the arrow is the slot's source**.
- **`20261022170000` adds `home_feeder_id` / `home_feeder_bracket` and the away pair** to `tdc_tournament_draw`. Round and position were enough to *label* a slot but not to draw to it: they do not say which bracket, so a losers-bracket slot fed from the main bracket was ambiguous. Built by patching the live definition, not retyping it.
- **One SVG over the whole bracket**, not borders between fixtures — an arrow crosses between columns, and an inserted empty round means it may cross more than one.
- **`pointer-events` is off on the layer and on for the paths alone**, so an arrow passing over a fixture never eats a click meant for it. The arrows sit at `z-index: 0` and the fixtures at `1`, so a line disappears behind a box the way a printed draw sheet looks. Verified with `elementFromPoint`: an arrow is still clickable where nothing covers it.
- **Clicking an arrow opens the wiring panel** for that slot's fixture. One panel for both slots rather than a control in each row — UV, 21 Oct, had the swap button covering the names, and a third control per row would repeat it.
- **The wiring panel rehearses every changed slot before asking once.** Two slots changed is two rehearsals and one confirmation, not two.
- **Arrow states:** solid when somebody is in the slot, faint and dashed while pending, dotted for `loser_of`, and the whole layer goes dashed when the tournament's progression is `manual` — every arrow is then something the TD will do by hand. An arrow pointing backwards into a lower round is legal and is **marked rather than hidden**.
- **Redrawn on render and on resize**, coalesced into an animation frame. No scroll handler: the SVG is inside the scrolling content and moves with it. A measurement taken while the bracket is 0×0 is skipped rather than drawn at the origin.

#### Rounds the page now draws
- **`1..max`, not the distinct rounds that have fixtures.** An inserted round is a gap in the numbering and the gap *is* the round. Skipping it would make the insert invisible and the round after it look like it moved for no reason.
- **The border between two rounds is a column of its own**, 0.75rem wide and widening to 2.25rem on hover to reveal a "+". A column cannot land on top of a fixture, which an overlay could, and it scrolls with the bracket. Verified: opacity 0 and 12px at rest, opacity 1 and 36px hovered.
- **A round on the end is a plain "+ Round"**, because adding there moves nothing — `tdc_add_fixture` takes it. Inserting below the top is `tdc_insert_round`, which renumbers and therefore confirms first.
- **`roundNames()` counts matches per round** instead of measuring distance from the end. Walking down from the last round: one match is the Final, two is the semis, four the quarters, and naming **stops the moment the halving breaks**. Distance was only ever right for a clean power-of-two ladder, which a prelim round destroys — it would have called the real quarter-finals the semis.

#### A bug this found
`render()` called `roundRulesButton(rest[0].bracket, …)` in the third-place branch. **`rest` does not exist in that scope** — a TD opening a bracket with a third-place playoff got a `ReferenceError` and no bracket at all. Introduced 22 Oct with the round-rules button and only caught while reading the same function to add the arrows; UV's test tournaments had no third-place playoff. Fixed to `third[0]`.

### One copy of the settings form (22 Oct)
`tournament-new.js` carried its own `control()` and its own "which settings are live" filter, written before `TDCMatchDef` exported either and identical to them bar the element-id prefix. Two copies of the form builder meant a setting added to `TDCMatchDef` would appear on the bracket's round panel and silently not on the create page.
- Removed. The page now calls `D.control(d, rules, 'tr')` and `D.liveRuleKeys(rules)`; the `'tr'` prefix keeps every element id exactly as it was.
- Verified by building the form both ways in headless Chromium and comparing `innerHTML`: **identical**, 12 controls, same ids. A dedup that changes nothing is the only kind worth doing without being asked.
- **Not** deduped: `setValue()` clamps an out-of-range number to the bound, while the round panel's `readValue()` rejects it with a message. That is a real behavioural difference, not drift, so it was left alone rather than quietly changed.
